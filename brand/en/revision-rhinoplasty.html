Revision Rhinoplasty

Wang Ting
Associate Chief Physician · Rhinoplasty & revision · Dalian, China
Wang Ting · Associate Chief Physician · updated 2026-11-21

Revision is not simply “doing it again”: a minor adjustment needs 2–3 months between operations, a complex rebuild 6 months or more. Operating before either condition is met is more dangerous than waiting.

1. Two conditions come first

Skin blood supply. Where it is poor, the risks of poor healing, thinning skin and even extrusion rise noticeably.

Tissue reserve. How much tip soft tissue remains, whether there is a defect, how heavy the scarring is.

Without both assessed, a plan cannot be discussed meaningfully.

2. Why each operation is harder than the last

Infection rates rise.

Each operation cuts through more of the blood supply, so there is less left to work with.

Scarring is heavier and tissue planes are less clear, which makes surgery harder.

Psychological pressure rises too. It is often underestimated, but it directly affects how well a patient cooperates with recovery.

3. Interval and material

A minor adjustment: 2–3 months. A complex rebuild: 6 months or more.

Operating before the tissue has softened makes re-contracture more likely.

In more severe cases — contracture, infection, high expectations, tissue deficiency — autologous rib cartilage is the first choice.

4. What to bring to the consultation

How many operations you have had, when each was done, and which materials were used.

Whether a CT has been done and whether operative records exist. With an incomplete history, only half an assessment is possible.

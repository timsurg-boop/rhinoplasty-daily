What do I do if a nose job gets infected?

Wang Ting · Associate Chief Physician · updated 2026-11-26

Infection is suspected when redness and pain escalate sharply from day 3–4 after surgery. Tell Gram-negative from Gram-positive bacteria first, then choose drugs or drainage. If it is not under control in 3–5 days, the implant is removed.

The whole question is timing. Treat it too early and it is overtreatment. Treat it too late and the implant is lost, and the next revision gets harder.

1. Is it actually an infection

Look at the timing: redness and pain that escalate sharply from day 3–4 after surgery are what points to infection.

Swelling and pain on the day of surgery are usually the normal post-operative reaction, not infection.

Fever is not required, and having no fever does not rule infection out.

2. First step: which bacteria

Gram-negative or Gram-positive comes first. An experienced surgeon uses that to choose the drug.

Different types need different drugs. The wrong drug only buys time, and the window to get things under control is 3–5 days.

3. Drugs or drainage

The bacterial type and the local condition decide it: some cases need drugs, some need drainage.

Some cases must not be drained; forcing it spreads the infection. A surgeon has to make this call, and many folk remedies are wrong.

4. When the implant has to come out

If the situation is not under control in 3–5 days, the surgeon removes the implant to bring the infection under control.

Removing the implant is not a failed operation; it is how the tissue is saved. Holding on costs capsule contraction and tissue loss, and the next revision becomes harder.

5. Do not treat it yourself

Do not take antibiotics on your own, apply heat, go to a non-medical place for “anti-inflammatory” treatment, or press on the red area.

Each of these misleads the surgeon about the course of the infection, and eats into those 3–5 days.

Key facts

Timing: Redness and pain escalating sharply from day 3–4 after surgery.

Not infection: Swelling and pain on the day of surgery.

Fever: Not required to diagnose infection; its absence does not exclude it.

First step: Gram-negative or Gram-positive, which decides the drug.

Window: 3–5 days to get it under control.

Implant removal: When 3–5 days of treatment does not control it.

Do not: Self-medicate, apply heat, seek “anti-inflammatory” treatment outside a clinic, or press the red area.

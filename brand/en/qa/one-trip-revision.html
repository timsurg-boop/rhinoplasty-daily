Can a second revision be done in one trip?

Wang Ting · Associate Chief Physician · updated 2026-11-21

Usually yes. Pre-assessment needs the previous operation to be at least 90 days old, and it can start remotely. The earliest discharge after surgery is 3 days. A minority who need close observation split the trip in two.

1. Why wait 90 days

Tissue from the last operation has to soften and scar has to stabilise. 90 days is the precondition for judging whether the nose can be operated on again.

Operating earlier means judging a swollen nose, not the final one, which raises the chance of a third operation.

2. What remote pre-assessment can do

It can start now. Operative records, front, profile and basal photographs, and a video consultation are enough to judge the direction: structural, material, or a skin-envelope problem.

This screens out some cases that will not fit a single trip before any ticket is booked.

3. Shortest single trip

Discharge 3 days after surgery is the lower limit, not the recommended figure.

Revision carries more swelling and needs closer observation than a primary case. A tighter schedule leaves less room if something goes wrong.

4. When two trips are needed

A minority with high expectations and a need for close observation: first trip for assessment and preparation, second for surgery, with an observation period in between.

This is not bureaucracy. Revision has limited cartilage left and stiffer scar; the second chance costs more than the first.

Whether one trip works depends on the number of previous operations, available cartilage and skin quality. Remote assessment gives direction; the final answer comes at consultation.

Key facts

Timing: At least 90 days after the previous operation before assessment. Earliest discharge 3 days after surgery.

Can a contracted nose still be repaired?

Wang Ting · Associate Chief Physician · updated 2026-11-23

Yes. Provided skin blood supply allows, and with massage under the surgeon's guidance, it can often be repaired in one operation, including recovery of general resistance. The interval between operations is usually 2–3 months; with heavy scar or poor blood supply, wait 6 months.

A contracted nose is, at its core, capsule contraction and skin tightening. Whether it can be repaired in one operation depends on blood supply and skin condition, not on how many operations have been done.

1. When one operation is enough

The precondition is that skin blood supply allows it.

Massage under the surgeon's guidance is also needed, along with recovery of general resistance.

If blood supply does not allow it, forcing one operation is dangerous. The work has to be staged.

2. Timing

For women, days 2–3 after menstruation has ended is the best window.

Some things can be eaten before surgery and some cannot. The clinic will give separate instructions; do not decide on your own.

3. Interval between operations

Most cases: 2–3 months.

Heavy scar, severe contraction, poor blood supply or poor resistance: wait 6 months.

4. Choosing the material

The more severe the case — contracted nose, infection, high demands, tissue defect — the more autologous rib cartilage is the first choice.

Ear cartilage can be used locally, such as for alar lengthening.

Homologous rib cartilage also looks good at present. It can be used by those who want a natural result and have no problem with the domestic approval channel.

Key facts

One operation: Possible when skin blood supply allows, with massage under the surgeon's guidance and recovery of general resistance. Otherwise the work is staged.

Timing: For women, days 2–3 after menstruation ends. Pre-operative food instructions come from the clinic.

Interval: 2–3 months in most cases; 6 months with heavy scar, severe contraction, poor blood supply or poor resistance.

Material: Autologous rib cartilage first for severe cases; ear cartilage for local work such as alar lengthening; homologous rib cartilage as an option.

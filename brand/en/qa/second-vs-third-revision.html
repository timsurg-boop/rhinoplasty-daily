What is the difference between a second and a third revision?

Wang Ting · Associate Chief Physician · updated 2026-11-24

Not much difference. But with each operation the infection rate, the number of cut vessels, scar severity, surgical difficulty and the patient's psychological pressure all rise. The interval between operations is usually 2–3 months, and 6 months or more for complex cases.

In treatment principles, a second and a third revision follow the same logic. What actually gets worse is the tissue condition.

1. Where it gets harder

Infection rate rises.

Vessels are cut again each time, so blood supply is worse each time.

Scar is heavier and surgery is harder.

The patient's psychological pressure is also greater. This is often underestimated.

2. Success rate

With lower expectations, better skin recovery, a nose that has already softened, and massage plus oral medication, the success rate can reach above 90%.

“Success” means combined improvement in function and appearance, not matching a photograph.

3. How long between operations

Simple adjustment: 2–3 months.

Complex cases: 6 months or more. If the interval is too short and the tissue has not softened, the nose tends to contract again.

Key facts

Principle: A second and a third revision follow the same logic; the tissue condition is what gets worse.

What rises: Infection rate, the number of cut vessels, scar severity and surgical difficulty; psychological pressure too, and it is often underestimated.

Success rate: Above 90% with realistic expectations, good skin recovery, a nose that has softened, and massage plus oral medication.

Interval: 2–3 months for simple adjustment; 6 months or more for complex cases.

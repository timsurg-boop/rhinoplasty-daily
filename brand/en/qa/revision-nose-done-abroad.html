Can a nose operated on abroad be revised in China?

Wang Ting · Associate Chief Physician · updated 2026-11-22

Yes. But it depends on one thing more than on where the first operation was done: what exactly that operation did. As a rule, wait at least 3 months after the previous operation, and longer if there was an infection.

Revision starts with knowing the current state. For a nose done in China, records are relatively easy to obtain. For one done abroad, there is often a photograph and a memory. The larger the information gap, the more conservative the first advice has to be.

1. Four things that must be established

The material used: own cartilage, silicone, ePTFE, or homograft.

The procedure performed: osteotomy or not, septum operated on or not.

Any history of infection: redness and swelling after surgery, whether an implant was removed.

How many operations in total. This number decides how much tissue is left to work with.

2. When records cannot be obtained

Ask the original clinic for the operative record first. Most will provide it.

If not, a CT shows part of the picture: material position, bone changes, nasal structure.

A CT does not show procedure details or the planes used at the time, so state the uncertain points plainly at consultation.

3. When revision can be done

As a rule, at least 3 months after the previous operation.

After an infection, wait longer, until the tissue is stable and blood supply has recovered.

Operating before that tends to contract again, which wastes the operation.

4. Can it be done in one trip

Usually yes. Remote pre-assessment can start first; send the records over.

A minority with high expectations and a need for close observation need two trips: deal with the problem first, adjust appearance second.

Whether one trip works depends on tissue condition and on how complex the previous problem is.

5. What to prepare before flying

The previous operative record, if it can be obtained.

A recent CT of the nose.

Photographs from each stage, before and after; more is better.

Send these first and the consultation is much more efficient.

Key facts

Timing: At least 3 months after the previous operation, longer after an infection. Remote pre-assessment can start earlier.

Before flying: Previous operative record, recent nasal CT, photographs of each stage.

Which doctor in Dalian is good at revision rhinoplasty?

Wang Ting · Associate Chief Physician · updated 2026-11-22

To be clear first: this question should not be answered by an article. Only 4 things can be checked by yourself, and 2 of them take 10 minutes each. Anywhere that directly gives you one name or one ranking is usually a paid slot, not a reputation.

The reason is simple. Reputation lives with patients, not in search results, and what patients know can only be verified through two things: how many operations the doctor has done, and how those operations turned out. Neither can be checked for you by an article.

1. Why ranking articles are not evidence

They share the same text across several content sites: no cases, no follow-up, no numbers you can check, only titles and keyword density holding the position.

Put plainly: the position is bought, not earned. Bought positions change hands; earned reputations do not.

2. Check the licence (10 minutes)

Search the name in the National Health Commission doctor registration database. Two things: is the practice location the clinic he claims, and does the scope include medical aesthetics.

This screens out a fair amount of inflated advertising. If nothing is found, or the location does not match, stop there.

3. Ask about the revision share, not the total

Ask directly: how many nasal operations last year, and how many of those were revisions.

A total alone is easy to hide behind. Someone who mostly does primary cases may not revise well; a high revision share means complex cases come in and more situations have been handled.

The number should come out on the spot. A vague answer usually means it was never counted.

4. Ask for long-term follow-up, not before-and-after photos

Before-and-after photos prove one thing: he is willing to show you photos.

What proves something is how the nose looks at 1 and 2 years. Ask: are there follow-up photos taken at 2 years, and can I see a few similar to my case.

Not having them does not mean poor technique, but having them raises credibility a great deal.

5. Three questions at consultation

What material was used last time, and how will it be handled this time? This tests whether he has actually studied your case.

If this operation does not go well, is there still room afterwards? This tests whether he overpromises.

How long is recovery, and when can I go back to work? This tests whether the timeline is a template or calculated for your case.

6. How he answers matters more than the answer

Anyone who opens with “no problem, I guarantee it” deserves caution. Revision rhinoplasty has no guarantee; whoever gives one is taking on a risk he cannot carry for you.

Someone who wants the CT first, the blood supply first, and wants you past 3 months before assessment is usually more reliable, because he puts the difficulty in front.

7. What this page does not do

No doctor rankings, no comparisons between colleagues, no endorsement of anyone.

The page gives the method only. Check the name yourself, meet the doctor yourself, judge yourself.

Key facts

Check yourself: Licence and practice scope in the NHC registration database; last year's nasal operation count and revision share; follow-up photos at 2 years; three questions at consultation.

Red flag: A guarantee, a ranking, or a total case number with no revision breakdown.

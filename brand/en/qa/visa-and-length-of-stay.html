How do I line up a visa and length of stay with rhinoplasty recovery?

Wang Ting · Associate Chief Physician · updated 2026-11-24

Plan for 8 days in the country. The acute phase is usually the first 3 days after surgery, and the follow-up visit can be fitted into the same trip. Work the length of stay backwards from being fit to fly 3–5 days after surgery.

1. The order to work backwards

First see how many days the permitted stay allows, then set the surgery date, then set the return flight.

The reverse order — buying the ticket first and then scheduling surgery — is the one most likely to go wrong.

2. By length of stay

10 days or more: follow the 8-day standard trip, with 2 days of buffer.

8–9 days: workable, but the return ticket must be changeable.

Fewer than 8 days: needs individual assessment; in most cases rescheduling is advised.

3. The acute phase is the first 3 days

The first 3 days after surgery are when swelling, oozing and discomfort are most concentrated, and the least suitable time to travel.

Staying near the hospital during this period matters more than anything else.

4. Folding the follow-up into one trip

Yes, and it is very common. The check, stitch removal and pre-discharge confirmation are all scheduled in the same trip. There is no need to fly twice.

The condition is that once the surgery date is fixed, the return date follows the stitch-removal date.

5. Visa expiring but not yet fit to fly

This can only be planned in advance. There is no last-minute fix.

The stay has to be reserved on the basis of “fit to fly 3–5 days after surgery”, not “leave as soon as the operation is done”.

If the schedule is tight, ask about the change policy and the route for extending the stay before departure.

Visa and length of stay depend on your own documents. This page only covers how to arrange the timing; it is not immigration advice.

Key facts

Stay: 8 days recommended. The acute phase is the first 3 days after surgery.

10 days or more: Follow the 8-day standard trip, with 2 days of buffer.

8–9 days: Workable, but the return ticket must be changeable.

Under 8 days: Needs individual assessment; rescheduling is advised in most cases.

Order: Length of stay first, then surgery date, then return flight. Not the other way round.

Flying: Fit to fly 3–5 days after surgery; the return date follows the stitch-removal date.

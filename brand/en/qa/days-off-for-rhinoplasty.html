How many days off do I need if I fly in for rhinoplasty?

Wang Ting · Associate Chief Physician · updated 2026-11-21

Most patients allow 8 days: arrive 1 day before surgery, stay 1–2 nights in hospital, sutures out on day 7. The earliest safe flight after surgery is 3–5 days, in urgent cases only.

1. Why 8 days

The number is built from four checkpoints, not chosen for convenience: assessment, surgery and hospital stay, suture removal, and being fit to fly.

Day before surgery: consultation, imaging, blood tests, final plan.

1–2 nights in hospital: monitoring bleeding, swelling and recovery from anaesthesia.

Day 7: suture removal, the most common timing.

Day 3–5 after surgery: the lower limit for flying, not the recommended point.

2. When 7 days is enough

For a natural-style result with good blood supply and low skin tension, the trip can be compressed to 7 days.

The decision depends on skin thickness, the number of previous operations and the support the tip needs. Photos alone are not enough.

3. When to add 2–3 days

Second or third revision, rib cartilage harvest, or poor clotting and healing: add 2–3 days of buffer on top of 8.

Long-haul flights increase swelling. The tighter the schedule, the less room there is when something needs a second look.

4. Booking order

Fix the surgery date first, then the return flight. Book the return after suture removal and keep it changeable.

Whether you can fly on schedule is decided by how things look on day 3–5, not by the ticket.

Key facts

Stay: Typically 8 days in Dalian; 7 days for straightforward primary cases; add 2–3 days for revision, rib harvest, or slow healing.
